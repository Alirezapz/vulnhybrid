VulnHybrid graph study and all-family fusion

1 November 2026 — version 1.2.0. Research artifact; not peer reviewed.

Main result

The primary validation-MCC-selected configuration is x_ggnn_sum_port. On the 2,500-function commit-disjoint test cohort it reaches accuracy 52.84%, F1 0.6158, MCC 0.0616 and average precision 0.5649. Token LR under the same validation-MCC policy reaches accuracy 50.96% and MCC 0.0565. The selected model's MCC difference is 0.0051, with 95% commit-cluster interval [-0.0916, 0.1161]. The conditional clustered interval does not establish a positive MCC gain.

This does not establish a universal best detector or world-first algorithm. It is evidence about these configurations, representations, labels and fixed cohorts. The selected method was chosen by validation, not by the largest test row. Historical v1 F1-selected numbers are retained separately; changing the threshold objective is not a new model improvement.

Executed scope and complete evidence

  • 54 baseline neural training runs: nine configurations on four cohorts, with three initialization/shuffle seeds on the primary cohort.
  • 24 additional neural runs: edge/payload/size controls, evidence-focused and operator/field-aware representations, relation gating, perturbation consistency, and architectural ports of typed GGNN/readout.
  • 32 selected classical graph models, including node histograms, AST/typed/CFG/last-identifier WL features and structural boosting.
  • 18 learned or fixed fusion recipes per cohort, including all-family stacking, convex mixtures, rank stacking, boosting, anchored residuals and confidence/quality gating. Every available informative executed expert can enter the all-family recipes; no unexecuted LLM is simulated.
  • 267 scored configuration/cohort comparisons across the four matched test sets. This count includes earlier baselines, seed repeats, ensemble configurations and ablations; it is not a count of that many distinct algorithms. F1/MCC operating points are reported separately, not counted as independent models.
  • Every method is also scored on frozen SVEN and verified PrimeVul external pairs. Raw scores, IDs, fit/selection membership, seeds, best epochs, histories, weights, timings, diagnostics and repaired implementation failures are retained. All scientific neural runs use the full training cohort.

The code includes original PyG GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN, GAT, R-GCN and GGNN operators, trained from scratch. Devign-style and typed architecture ports are adaptations on a shared frontend. Full end-to-end Devign, ReVeal, IVDetect, DeepWukong and AMPLE paper reproductions are not claimed. Their original repositories and exactness requirements are listed in GRAPH_REPLICATION_LEDGER. No finite registry can contain every graph method published in the field. The supplemental 2025–2026 review also covers HAGNN, LLMxCPG, VulGNN and Vul-CTG, with explicit reading depth and unexecuted status.

Input and protocol integrity

All four old cohort memberships and validation halves are unchanged. Old text/static predictions were independently reproduced within 1e-7 before extending them to the new external code. Train-only TF-IDF, validation-only calibration, and separate validation selection remain enforced. Graph features contain no IDs, labels, CWE/CVE metadata, filenames or commit identity.

Primary neural budgets are shared: 48 hidden units, two conventional message layers or three ordinary gated steps; eight typed gated steps for the inspected Devign architectural port; batch 128; AdamW .001, weight decay .0001; up to eight epochs with patience two and minimum three epochs. Checkpoints maximize val_fit AP. Prototype and cross-project runs use seed 42. This is a bounded CPU budget, not exhaustive architecture/hyperparameter optimization.

The extension reuses previously seen v1 test cohorts. The graph protocol was frozen before full graph fitting; a representation addendum was driven by input coverage before inspecting graph test metrics. The entire extension remains exploratory, not an externally preregistered independent confirmation.

Representation audit

The base graph contains tree-sitter named AST nodes and leaf payload, next-leaf links, last-identical-identifier links and a structured statement CFG. The latter two are approximate: they are not sound pointer-aware reaching definitions or a compiler CPG. Switch, goto and exception semantics are diagnosed, not solved. The base named-node input omits unnamed operators and AST field roles; a matched richer control preserves those. Edge-only ablations share the node set.

Across 28164 Devign/SVEN inputs, tree-sitter reports error nodes in 5288 functions and the 256-node representation truncates 9458. Errors and truncation stay in the full comparison. A common error-free/non-truncated sensitivity subset is separately recorded.

Perturbation and operating-point analyses

Frozen models are evaluated with edges removed, relation-wise rewiring, anonymous leaf payload and node permutation. These are diagnostic input perturbations, not all semantics-preserving program edits. No threshold is retuned on perturbed test data. DeepSets is a topology-free control; ordinary GNN pooling is permutation-invariant, whereas convolutional node-axis readout can depend on order. The complete score changes, flip rates and MCC are in stability_metrics.csv.

Both F1-selected and MCC-selected operating points are preserved for every configuration. Low-FPR points are selected at observed val_select caps of .5%, 1% and 5%, with actual test FPR/recall reported. The caps are not deployment guarantees. Review precision/recall at fixed 1/5/10/20% budgets is also saved, with the expected random-review baseline. No LLM cost or human-review time is inferred from these offline rankings.

Statistics, reproducibility and publication scope

Test intervals and paired differences use 1,000 shared commit-cluster bootstrap resamples. McNemar tests are secondary accuracy tests with Holm adjustment, not tests of F1/MCC; sample dependence remains a limitation. Intervals are conditional on the fitted/selected models and omit split/training uncertainty.

The model archive preserves trained weights and classical estimators. Raw per-function scores and frozen memberships are in the experiment-data archive; code, configs and compact tables are tracked in Git. Regenerate graph caches from the pinned data with the documented extraction commands. Direct environment versions and input/protocol hashes are recorded. Tests cover CFG returns, parse diagnostics, edge validity, operator preservation, every architecture's gradients, topology controls and meta-data leakage. A separate verifier recomputes scores/selections from exported prediction files.

This provides a substantive graph/fusion research artifact. Publication claims still require exact named baseline pipelines on their intended representations, stronger chronological/near-clone controls, more split/training seeds and manual real-project validation. No unrun method receives an invented score, and no acceptance/SOTA/world-first claim follows from the experiment count.
